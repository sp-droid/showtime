Interesting studies

Edel et al. (2024), “Metabolic profile in elite badminton match play and training drills,” European Journal of Sport Science.

This study made professional players play for 15+15 minutes with a 5 minute rest in between, measuring their energy consumption split into 3 categories:

Energy system Contribution
Oxidative/aerobic 56.9 ± 8.6%
Phosphocreatine 42.7 ± 8.7%
Anaerobic glycolysis 0.4 ± 0.6%

A short summary on the differences is this:

  • The phosphocreatine pathway can cover the most intense efforts, but not for too long (a few dozen seconds)
  • Aerobic provides a slow release of energy pulled from body reserves
  • Anaerobic provides energy for something in between. Very hard efforts that demand too much ATP for aerobic metabolism but last too long for phosphocreatine to cover all of it.

During short 10-30 s rallies, they found glycolysis only contributed 1-2%, whereas in longer 50 s it could rise to around 12%. They also found there was no significant difference between the first and second set.

What this means is that badminton is too intense to rely fully on aerobic metabolism, but not long enough to switch to anaerobic consistently. So, how do we optimize our training knowing this? I don't know the exact answer to this yet. Perhaps find time to train in a way that you can give your absolute maximum effort, and recover just in time to do it again without fatigue, with the same intensity.

Personal opinions

Equipment

Summary for a new person that wants to train seriously. You will learn to change some stuff on your journey, it's pointless to want to be perfect at the beginning:

Racket Shuttles String Grips Shoes Socks Clothing
After 30-40$, don't obsess. Feathers only, up to budget. 20-27 lbs, don't obsess. Buy base grip once, protect it with cheaper over-grips / towel grips. Indoor sport ones, insoles are good and cheap Indoor sport socks, worth it Worth it. Proper shirt, perhaps something to manage sweat getting on hands and eyes.

  • Racket. The really cheap rackets are trash, not worth buying. But anything over 20 - 30 € is fine to play, as long as you can string it to the tension you are accustomed to. The limit is on the racket. There are caveats. The only difference that you can notice is the way the shaft bends on hard shots, and the weight category that impacts manoeuvrability.

    The material used also matters, for example to play with people you don't have synergy with, a carbon fibre racket on a clash will break (like mine did. It was a good one, and I'm still traumatised), but an aluminium alloy one probably won't. I still play with my first racket ever, from this material, a thousand scratches, frame hits, racket clashes, strung to 26-28 lbs and still going.

    Having said that, for singles and doubles (if you trust your partner well) once you get good it makes total sense to invest a little more.

  • Shuttle. I'd rather play with a broken feather than the best plastic shuttle. Most expensive part of the sport, but it really matters, otherwise the flight path is off, you can't slice it, net spins are different... Plastic skirts also deform on heavy smashes which turns them into bullets, more dangerous and games devolve into permanently smashing. New synthetic ones like the Victor Carbonsonic series are really close in behaviour but unfortunately the carbon fibre tips are a serious hazard.

  • String. This is sort of like noticing if your racket bends on impact. Until that point there is not really a justification for getting something different, because you are not good enough. Me for example I think I can't notice anything better from my 30 € racket bought 7 years ago strung with BG65, I'm not there yet.

    The tension at which you string it however... is noticeable. Just don't do big jumps in tension because it increases the shock our hand-elbow-shoulder joints go through, you can get injured. They also break more easily on mishits. You can start with 22 or so and up it a bit every time you restring it. 24, 25, 26, 27...

  • Base grips. Only change to towel grip if you try and like the feeling, otherwise there is no need to change the base grip of the racket. You don't even need to replace it if you put an over-grip and replace that instead.

  • Over-grips. Once I started getting a bit better my hand struggled with the thin base grip over the wood, it hurts. Putting a cheap over-grip really improved my feel and eliminated any pain. Once it becomes slippery some months later just replace it.

  • Books, manuals... It's already difficult to put badminton technique into words, and it's even harder to learn from it so don't pay. I bought a small one and didn't gain anything substantial. You have free guides in video format in YouTube, free articles, forum discussions... Coaches, practise and recording yourself is better than most of that anyway.

  • Shoes. I'm not a big fan of dedicated shoes. I have some volleyball ones though. They do have more durability, which is enough for me to have them. I noticed they grip much more, which I strongly dislike, so I never clean the soles with water like some do. It's probably just me, because I believe that stopping your body should mainly come from stability and body control, not a shoe that sticks to the ground. I did some investigating and everything pointed towards higher ACL, patellar and ankle injuries from the greater peak braking forces. I'm not the best person to ask in this area because my opinion goes against what 99% of people will tell you.

  • Insoles. Pretty good. More cushioning, you can replace them more often, makes shoes last longer... I tried sport gel and normal harder ones, the harder ones perform well.

  • Socks. This was a game changer. Normal socks aren't ready for badminton. Too thin, too slippery inside the shoe. Pretty happy with Yonex socks.

  • Clothing. Indoor sports t-shirt, dry-fit style works well. If you sweat a lot consider wristbands to stop sweat getting into the hand, and headbands to stop it from getting into your eyes.

Training

  • Stop playing if you notice lasting pain while playing. When recovering, focus on improving technique, you are most likely doing something wrong.
  • Warm up legs.
  • Eat and drink after training for better recovery.
  • Eat big meals +3-4 hours before at least, to avoid stomach upset.
  • Have a small snack 1 h before, like a banana, to not feel empty.
  • If it's cold outside, wear gloves on the main hand for the walk so your hand is warm before.
  • Keep the racket up
  • In doubles, if you are in front, don't look back. If you are behind, look where your teammate is.

Progress

The first time I ever touched a badminton racket happened during a physical education class in high school, just to learn the rules and play with each other over a few weeks. I really liked it. Next time was during my bachelors in university in 2018, I bought a badminton racket for 30 € and played every now and then with my friends. I tried looking for lessons and I did join one a couple times but it was pretty basic. I should've searched for a club. Then in 2024 I finally did, at 15zero in Milan, Italy, during my master's degree.

Summary

Q1Q2 2026 — Long breaks from work and a left-shoulder injury; rehab and return to play; started training with Camilla’s group at Palabadminton; Bacone course felt socially unwelcoming; own training, power, cardio, and reaction work improving; won a singles match in Florence after a close three-set battle; need much more 1v1 practice.

Q4 2025 — Joined first competitive course; stronger training environment and good coaching; entered two singles tournaments and lost first round; noticeable progress by December; working on posture, shot selection, and doubles rotation; solid Christmas social tournament; improving rear-court backhand and experimenting with training.

Q3 2025 — Milan summer badminton: heat, mosquitoes, rain, suffering. 🔥🦟☔😓

Q1Q2 2025 — Basic adult course had limited value; improved precision and technique through stronger free-play opponents; hip injury badly affected lunges for months; recovered in time for the summer social tournament; won 7/9 games but missed the podium after one poor result.

Q4 2024 — Switching to feather shuttles helped a lot; found real enjoyment in badminton; good natural speed from weight training despite poor footwork; steady gains in precision and doubles game sense; improved emotional control and sportsmanship.

Before Sep 2024 — Generally weak level; main long-term improvement was racket feel and comfort.

Injury log

My ankle has gone into hyper-extension quite a few times while playing but never got anything more than a scare. I can roll my ankle and put a lot of weight on it so I'll probably be fine on this at least.

Mid 2026 - Left shoulder (I'm right-handed) acute strain. It wasn't related to badminton I think, but it made me skip 3 weeks. I couldn't sleep without a throbbing pain at some point, used ibuprofen for 1 week (first oral, then topical)... I could not move my arm 1 single bit towards the front or side, ok towards the back.

Late 2025 - Small strain on my index finger ligament responsible of stabilising it when it moves sideways. Hurt when holding a heavy pan for example, because the weight rests on the index laterally. Healed in a month after soft racket drills first and then isometric finger holds.

Early 2025 - Hit near my eye, on my temple (zygomatic bone) with a racket frame. Positioning mistake and lack of awareness from my teammate ended up in me getting hit. I had to wait for a couple minutes to regain composure at it really hurt, the area was tender for 2 weeks.

Early 2025 - Strain on my hips. I got over-training issues after going from 4 h to 12 h of play every weekend, combined with no hip strengthening on my routine and technique problems. Didn't heal for 3-4 months while playing. I tried many things, but what healed it was active rest. Hip flexion, extension, abduction and adduction, with increasing light weights and skipping a weekend without playing.

Early 2021 - Spontaneous pneumothorax two or three days after diving on concrete floor, playing with friends. I had had surgery on both lungs when I was 15 and 17, and therefore this shouldn't have happened, so I think that me being on the thinner side, having little muscle and the impact on the floor loosened some stuff. I had 3 on my right lung over the next months, did a CT scan and revealed many lung bullae on the left one too. Since then I haven't had any, and I've had worse falls. It's just a particular issue of mine I had growing up.

Late 2018 - Strain on my shoulder rotator cuff from bad technique on overhead shots. Healed in 3 months on its own, not playing.

Training per week, through time

Q1Q2Q3 2026 - borderline sporadic, periods of playing 3 times a week, training on my own twice, periods of playing once per week.

Q4 2025 - 1.5 h of formal training, 7-8 in total, I'm trying to get all my gym days back, switch up my routine to single sets to true failure, full body every other day. In between heavy racket drills. I'm also trying to get at least 1 day of cardio in there, though I can see it improving already without it. Also added short isometrics (body-weight lunge, squat and fingertip plank) morning/night for tendon health. I take some collagen, protein and vitamin c every two days before the full body workouts.

Q3 2025 - Summer is very hard to play this in Milan. Scorching hot, humid, mosquitoes biting while I play... Only go a couple times in July-August.

Q1Q2 2025 - 12 h of play, 6 after injury, I skip many gym days. I start doing heavy racket drills.

Q4 2024 - 2-4 h of play, about 3 h of strength training
