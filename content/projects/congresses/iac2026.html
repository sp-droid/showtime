Cowell's formulation in cartesian coordinates

r¨=−μ⊕r3r+apert;y={r;v}

Why cartesian instead of equinoctial, KS 4D...

Very simple and it's what the source for the validation does, keeping it apples to apples. More in "Future Work" slide.

 

Fixed-step integration

  • Runge–Kutta, from Euler up to Prince–Dormand 7(8).
  • Low-storage RK CK2N54.
  • Yoshida symplectic splitting into kinetic and potential steps (orders 4, 6, 8).
  • Kepler splitting (Wisdom–Holman, SABA2–4), where the Kepler drift uses universal variables and only the perturbations are "kicks".

 

Reference frame and time

The frame is FK5 J2000 mean equator and equinox, the same as GMAT's EarthMJ2000Eq.

IAU-1976 precession, IAU-1980 nutation, GAST, and polar motion from IERS EOP C04.

Time scales: UTC→TAI with leap seconds, TT, and UT1 from EOP.

 

Perturbations

  • J2. Computed about the true Earth rotation pole, not the inertial z-axis.

aJ2=32J2μ⊕R⊕2r5[(5(k^⋅r)2r2−1)r−2(k^⋅r)k^]

a3b=∑j∈{Sun,Moon}μj(sj−r‖sj−r‖3−sj‖sj‖3)

  • Drag: MSISE-90 density

Some implementation optimizations

 

Double buffering

The propagation is broken down into chunks for two purposes:

  • It's possible to transfer one chunk of GPU data at the same time the other is being computed, so data transfer latency is hidden.
  • Chunks being moved to RAM means the GPU memory requirements can be as low as needed

 

Kahan compensated summation on the state update. Very long running sums with high dynamic range benefit from carrying error terms for extra accuracy.

 

Model optimization for different time scales

The pole direction from FK5 (used for J2 later) is computed on the CPU every i.e. 60 s, storing the pole before and after. The kernel interpolates it linearly instead of recomputing it.

 

Not everyone owns a datacenter GPU

Consumer GPUs have much lower FP64/FP32 ratios:

  • RTX 2060 1:32, 6.5 TFLOPS FP32
  • RTX 5090 1:64, 105 TFLOPS FP32
  • Apple M5 0:1, 41.1 TFLOPS FP32

Note: Apple GPUs don't even have FP64 native compute.

However, switching to FP32 as-is carries considerable penalties in accuracy due to the high dynamic range, making it unusable. CUDAjectory in its inception faced this problem and abandoned its implementation.

This is why orblib has developed in testing an FP32 variant that retains FP64 accuracy while being from 7x (1:32 devices) to 14x (1:64 ones) faster.

In fact, in our early testing, consumer hardware has come up on top in terms of performance per cost.

This was achieved using double FP32 pairs, explained in future talks.

Similar Works

What is the current tool ecosystem?

 

Direct numerical propagation for GPUs, no SGP4:

  • esa / CUDAjectory

TUDelft Márton Geda's MSc thesis, CUDAjectory is a numerical, high-fidelity propagation. Restricted to EEA.

As of 2026, to our knowledge, there is no peer-reviewed, publicly available GPU propagator for numerical integration of Earth orbits.

There are however projects on SGP4 and for CPU-only there are established tools such as STK, FreeFlyer, GMAT, Orekit...


orblib, in comparison to CUDAjectory:

  • Not mature
  • Not vendor-locked. While the numba-cuda-MLIR backend supports NVIDIA, the taichi backend supports other GPU vendors: Intel, AMD, Apple.
  • Not only FP64 (much better performance on consumer GPUs, can run on Apple FP32 restricted GPUs). Minor loss in accuracy.
  • Future release open-source outside of EEA
  • Not only GPU. Numba LLVM JIT backend supports x86 but also ARM64 CPUs. This includes parallel CPU propagation for both as well.
 

Minor works:

  • UtkarshJoshiNtl / Astrosis
  • adxorg / astrodynx

Minor GitHub projects, in experimental stages or not actively maintained anymore.


Other works

Picard–Chebyshev (parallel over time nodes):

  • Bai & Junkins, AAS/AIAA SFM 2010. Modified Chebyshev–Picard Iteration (MCPI) on NVIDIA GPUs (CUDA/cuBLAS), parallel over the nodes of a single trajectory.
  • Woollands, Bani Younes, Atallah & Young, JPL R&TD 2019. CUDA MCPI with high-fidelity gravity (200×200 EGM2008, plus a finite-element model) evaluated in parallel at all nodes.
  • Masat, Colombo & Boutonnet, Acta Astronautica 2023. Picard–Chebyshev "augmented" to batch many trajectories together with their nodes into large matrix operations, fully using the GPU.
  • Masat, Colombo & Boutonnet, ESA GNC 2023. Same solver with regularised formulations (Kustaanheimo–Stiefel) for near-singular and impact trajectories, fp64 Tensor Cores, and 50–100× faster than their previous version.

Parallel in time:

  • Rubio, Delgado, García-Gutiérrez & Escapa, Acta Astronautica 2025. Waveform relaxation with RK4 as an alternative to Picard–Chebyshev, designed for GPU parallelism; reports 20× over Picard–Chebyshev in Cartesian variables.

One thread per object, step-based (closest to orblib):

  • Arora & Russell, 2011. Early GPU catalogue propagation with Runge–Kutta.
  • Arora, Vittaldev & Russell, JGCD 2015. Multi-object GPU propagation with interpolated gravity and third-body models.
  • Rubio, Gonzalo, Siminski & Escapa, ASR 2024. CUDA geopotential (Cunningham) in fp64 and mixed precision; about 3,200 LEO objects, validated against GMAT, GODOT and Orekit.

Ongoing & Future Work

Coming soon on PyPI?

The package will be released in the future.

  • Optimized force modelling

Optimization of drag (60x), 3rd body (7.5x) and SRP (7.5x) is ongoing, specifically made for the GPU.

  • FP32 improvements

Most GPUs are far better at single precision compute, but a single FP32 number doesn't offer enough precision for the orbit problem.

A model using high+low FP32 pairs has been shown to recover most of the accuracy for considerable gains (7-14x).

  • Offer also models with the ICRF reference frame, a more complete geopotential, other perturbations...
  • Integrating in non-cartesian variables
  • Move from fixed-step integration

Having a similar number of steps per thread is important to avoid divergence and increase occupancy. For now it has been enforced by using integrators with a constant time step, but the actual condition is having the same number of steps in total. There's a gap there, could be exploited.

  • Integration error estimate
  • Collision detection
  • Real time plotting

Similar to how the lattice Boltzmann CFD solver "FluidX3D" is able to plot the simulation in real time because the data is already resident in the GPU, orblib would also be able to do so.

  • Running on multi-GPU nodes & multi-node setups
  • Streaming to disk directly
  • N-body and restricted N-body problems
